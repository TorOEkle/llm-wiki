# Indicator: Consumption (Household)

## Definition
Household consumption (Privat konsum/Hushållens konsumtion) measures the total spending by households on goods and services. It is the largest component of GDP in both countries.

## Historical & Forecasted Growth (Annual % Change)

| Year | Norway | Sweden |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2023** | -0.1% | -2.5% |

## Comparison/Analysis
- **The 2023 Dip**: Sweden saw a much sharper contraction in consumption (-2.5%) compared to Norway (-0.1%) as high inflation and interest rates hit Swedish households harder and earlier.
- **Recovery Paths**: Both countries expect a recovery in consumption as real wages start rising again. Norway's recovery is projected to be very strong in 2025 (2.8%) before stabilizing. Sweden's consumption is expected to pick up pace in 2026-2027 as interest rates remain low.

## Narrative: The Engine of Recovery
Household consumption has transitioned from a drag on growth in 2023 to a primary engine of recovery in 2025-2026. This shift is driven by the return of real wage growth after years of high inflation.

### Norway's Real Income Boost
In Norway, consumption growth has been bolstered by significant increases in disposable real income over the last two years.

> "To år med klar vekst i husholdningenes disponible realinntekt har bidratt til at veksten i forbruket har tatt seg opp." — [Nettrapport PPR 1/2026](../raw/Nettrapport_PPR_12026.md)

### Sweden's Domestic Demand Drive
In Sweden, despite the geopolitical shocks in early 2026, domestic demand—and specifically household consumption—remains the key driver of the GDP recovery.

> "Framöver drivs tillväxten av den inhemska efterfrågan. Stigande reala disponibla inkomster bedöms leda till att konsumtionen fortsätter att öka i god takt." — [Penningpolitisk rapport Mars 2026](../raw/penningpolitisk-rapport-mars-2026.md)
