# Indicator: Currency (Exchange Rates)

## Overview
Both the Norwegian Krone (NOK) and the Swedish Krona (SEK) are small currencies that have faced significant depreciation pressure against the EUR and USD in recent years.

## Development in Norway (I-44 Index)
*Note: Higher index value means a weaker currency.*

| Period | I-44 Index (Level) | Assessment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2023** | 120.3 | Significantly weak. |
| **2024** | 120.3 | Stabilized at a weak level. |
| **2025** | 119.6 | Slight strengthening. |
| **2026 (F)** | 113.2 | Expected strengthening due to high interest rate differential. |

## Development in Sweden
- The SEK has been under similar pressure. The Riksbank's decision to cut rates aggressively in 2025 was made despite the risk of further currency weakening, as domestic inflation fell below target.

## Comparison/Analysis
- **Interest Rate Differential**: Norway's higher interest rates (relative to Sweden and the Eurozone) are intended to support the NOK by making it more attractive for investors.
- **Commodity Link**: The NOK remains sensitive to oil and gas prices, while the SEK is more tied to global manufacturing and export demand.
